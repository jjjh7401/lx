Chapter 23

How the Console Works

From the values you enter to the stage

In this chapter
  • Get a single picture of the path a value you touch by hand takes — through the programmer, presets and cues, and sequences — to the executor.
  • Check, by color in the Track Sheet, tracking: a cue holds only what changed.
  • Avoid in advance the three things everyone gets stuck on the first day at the console.
Opening scene

Try editing a photo on your phone. While you raise the brightness and change the color, the photo on screen changes, but the original is still untouched.

The edit is saved only when you press “Done,” and if you do not like it halfway, you just press Cancel.

Now write a report in a word processor. If you gave every heading the “Heading 1” style, then changing the font of that one style later changes every heading in the report at once. If you had changed the font of each heading separately, you would have had to fix them one by one.

A lighting console works on these same two principles. There is a place where you touch things temporarily until you save, and there are references that call what you saved by name. If in Parts 1–4 you read the song and wrote the cue sheet, Part 5 is the process of moving that onto a grandMA3 console. This chapter is the first step: where a value passes inside the console on its way to the stage.

The Workflow on Site

On site, programming starts before the fixtures are hung on the real stage. You move the lighting plot into a simulation program, use that screen as the stage to build the cues, and run the whole song before rehearsal.

  1. Move the lighting plot and patch sheet into the simulation and the console (Sections 22.5–22.6 and 24.2).
  2. Patch on the console, and match the fixtures’ places and directions on the simulation screen (Chapter 24).
  3. Play the music and build presets, phasers and cues while watching the simulation screen (Chapters 24–28).
  4. Run the whole song to check it, and record it to watch with the director or the artist (Section 28.5).
  5. Fix at rehearsal the places where the real stage differs from the simulation (Chapter 30).

The exercises in this book use the 3D view built into onPC as the simulation. External simulation programs are summarized in Section 24.7.

23.1 The Data Flow

A value you touch by hand on grandMA3 passes through the programmer → presets and cues → sequences → executors in turn on its way to the stage. If you first learn just the meaning and order of these five, then for any other feature you only need to see where among them it attaches.

  • Programmer: the temporary memory where the values you are touching now sit. It is like the screen of a photo you are in the middle of editing. You see it on stage at once, but nothing is kept anywhere until you store.1
  • Preset: a value you will reuse, stored with a name. The palette colors settled in Part 2 (Pearl Pink, Lilac, Aqua) and the positions of Chapter 9 (Downlight, Rooftop Fan narrow, Cross Beam) go in here.
  • Cue: one stored scene. One line of the Chapter 16 cue sheet is one cue. A cue can hold values directly or refer to presets.2
  • Sequence: a list that holds cues in order, along with how to move from one cue to the next. One layer split out in Chapter 13 is one sequence.
  • Executor: the fader and button that run a sequence.2
Table 23-1 The five stages and what you made in earlier chapters
Console Holds What you made in earlier chapters
Programmer The value you are touching now (temporary) —
Preset Values to reuse (color, position, beam, effect) The Chapter 8 palette, the Chapter 9 positions, the shape of the Chapter 11 rhythm layers
Cue One scene One line of the Chapter 16 cue sheet
Sequence The order of cues and how they step The Chapter 13 sequence list (scene, back low bass (808), up moving …)
Executor The faders and buttons that run a sequence The Chapter 17 song page, the Chapter 21 busking page

When you open a new show file, sequence 1 is already attached to executor 201. Executors are numbered by page, and 201 is the first fader position of that page. After you make a value, press Store and then an empty executor button, and cue 1 of a new sequence is created and attached to that executor.3 At first this is the fastest way. On the command line you give the cue number directly, as in Store Cue 1, and decimal numbers such as 0.5 or 1.1 work too. That means you can carry the numbers of the Chapter 16 cue sheet over as they are.

23.2 Presets Are References, Not Copies

When you put a preset into a cue, the value is not copied; a mark pointing to the preset is stored. So, like the “Heading 1” style in the opening scene, when you fix one preset, every cue that uses it follows.4

This difference shows at rehearsal. Say the Pearl Pink of LOVE ATTACK looks too cold on stage. If you made Pearl Pink a preset and called it in every cue, fixing that one preset changes the Pearl Pink of the intro, verses, choruses and ending all at once. If you typed RGB values into every cue, you would have to find each cue that used the value and fix them one by one.

So the order is always the same. Make presets first, and make cues from presets. You make presets in Chapter 24 and cues in Chapter 27. What you fix at rehearsal is mostly not the cue but the preset.

Words to Know
Hard value
A value put straight into a cue without going through a preset. If a value is used in only that one cue, a hard value is fine.
Store and Update
Store saves something new; Update changes a cue or preset that already exists to the current values.

23.3 Tracking: Cues Hold Only What Changed

By default a grandMA3 cue stores only the values that changed from the previous cue, and the rest carry on as they are until another cue changes them. This is called tracking.5

Look again at the Chapter 16 cue sheet: in the “Scene” cell of cue 1.1 it says “Same,” and in cue 1.2 there is only one entry, “lower movers up one step.” Tracking is the console doing that “Same” by itself. Cue 1.1 holds only “back on at the first beat of the bar”; the wash at 30% and Pearl Pink come over from cue 1.

How tracking carries on is shown by color in the sequence’s Track Sheet.5

Table 23-2 The colors of the Track Sheet 5
Color Meaning
Magenta A value carried over from the previous cue (not stored in this cue)
White The same value as the previous cue, stored again in this cue (Blocked)
Green The dimmer is lower than in the previous cue
Cyan The dimmer is higher than in the previous cue

As convenient as tracking is, there is one thing to be careful about. A value from the previous cue can carry on to places you did not want. If a value should be used in one cue only and go back to what it was in the next, use the /CueOnly option when you store. The original value then goes into the next cue as Blocked, so the previous look does not leak (it cannot be used on the last cue of a sequence).6 The case where an effect stays on into the next cue is covered separately in Chapter 27.

A Short Experiment
  1. Open onPC, patch a few fixtures, store cue 1 with the dimmer at 50%, and store cue 2 changing only the color.
  2. In the Track Sheet of cue 2, see whether the dimmer cell is magenta.
  3. Now update the dimmer of cue 1 to 80% and call cue 2 again.
  4. If the dimmer comes out at 80% in cue 2 even though you did not touch it, you have seen tracking.

23.4 HTP, LTP and Priority

When two places touch the same attribute of the same fixture at once, the one with the higher priority wins, and if the priority is equal, the later value wins (LTP).

On grandMA3, between sequences whose priority is not HTP, the dimmer is also handled as LTP. When you want to combine dimmers by taking the higher value, set the priority of those sequences to HTP. Put into console terms, the principle you saw in Chapter 11 is this.

  • HTP (Highest Takes Precedence): the highest dimmer value wins. If two sequences with priority set to HTP turn on the same wash at 40% and 70%, you get 70% regardless of which came on later.
  • LTP (Latest Takes Precedence): the latest value wins. Color and position are always LTP, and between sequences whose priority is LTP (normal), the dimmer too goes to whichever came on later.7

Each sequence can be given a priority, and the order from highest is in Table 23-3.

Table 23-3 Sequence priority (highest first) 7
Order Name Meaning
1 Super Even above the programmer
2 Swap Treats the dimmer like LTP and sits above HTP
3 HTP The higher dimmer value wins
4–5 Highest, High Above normal
6 LTP Normal
7–8 Low, Lowest Below normal

Here is one rule that surprises people using it for the first time. On grandMA3, a playing sequence takes precedence over the programmer.1 If you select a fixture a sequence is running and change a value, that sequence sends the same attribute again and the value you entered may snap back. To make the programmer’s value win, turn on Freeze (the function that holds the programmer’s values even while a sequence runs). People coming from other consoles tend to know this the other way around.

In Chapter 13 we said the key stays outside the song’s sequences. On the console, put the key on a separate sequence or a fixed fader, and split things so that the song’s sequences do not touch the key fixtures’ dimmers. If a song cue still has to touch the key, give the key sequence a higher priority than the scene sequence. The default priority of a new sequence is not written in the manual, so check it yourself in the sequence settings window.[Author to confirm]

23.5 The Three Things You Get Stuck On First

What blocks you on the first day at the console is nearly always one of three things: how to clear the programmer, tracking, or priority. The table sorts them so that you can find the cause straight from the symptom.

Table 23-4 The three things you get stuck on first 1 5 8
Symptom Cause Fix
You stored, but the cue is empty You pressed Clear twice and then stored. The second Clear turns the values inactive while leaving them on stage, and only active values are stored Press Clear three times (or hold it for over a second, once) to clear everything, then build again
Strange values mix into the next cue After storing the previous cue you did not clear the programmer, so the leftover values were stored too Always clear the programmer entirely before making a new look and after storing (ClearAll)
The value you changed snaps back A playing sequence beats the programmer Turn on Freeze, or turn that sequence off and work

The Clear key does something different each time you press it. Once releases only the fixture selection, twice turns the values inactive, and three times clears everything.1 Because the values are still visible on stage at the second step, you end up feeling “there are values but they won’t store.”

There is one more place to get stuck when you work from the command line. Right after leaving the Patch screen, a command such as Fixture 201 Thru 212 (a command to select fixtures 201 through 212; Thru means “from … to …”) may be rejected with “Illegal object.” That is because the command line’s working location is still inside the patch. In that case, first enter ChangeDestination Root, which returns the working location to the very top (Root), and then type the command again. The author ran into this on onPC 2.4.2 and has not yet confirmed whether it is the same in 2.5.8 [Author to confirm]

What Changed in 2.5
  • Screen colors2.5 uses a new color theme made for dark control rooms. What is not selected looks dark and what you pick stands out. If the 2.4 look is familiar, the theme file (default_legacy.xml) can bring back the old colors.9
  • 3D viewThe graphics method changed from OpenGL to Vulkan. Before installing onPC, check that your PC meets the system requirements of 2.5.9
  • Sound in onPCThe default for audio input and output is None. To use the features that take in sound (Speed Master 16 in Chapter 28, the Sound and BPM triggers in Chapter 27), choose the input device yourself.9
  • Bug-fix releases2.5.1.0 and 2.5.1.1 fixed the length calculation of cues containing recipes, timecode events being recorded overlapping, and more. If you use 2.5, get the latest bug-fix release.10

23.6 [Exercise] LOVE ATTACK Cues 1, 1.1 and 1.2 on onPC

On grandMA3 onPC, make the first three lines of the Chapter 16 cue sheet into cues, and check tracking in the Track Sheet. The patch of all the fixtures is done in Chapter 24, so here we use only three groups.

Follow along

  1. Download grandMA3 onPC 2.5 (2.5.1.1 or later) from the MA Lighting website, install it, and open a new show file.
  2. In Patch, add 12 moving washes (201–212) and 16 moving beams (401–416), and store the 6 washes that take the back role as a separate group. Any model will do.
  3. Clear the programmer, select the 12 washes, and set the dimmer to 30% and the color to Pearl Pink (R 100, G 72, B 85). Leave the moving beams at dimmer 0, narrow toward the floor. Store it as cue 1 and name it “Intro · Pearl Heart.”
  4. Clear the programmer, select only the 6 back fixtures, and give them dimmer 50%. Store it as cue 1.1. (In a real show this pulse is handled not by the scene sequence but by the back low bass layer sequence, and the scene’s cue 1.1 is “Same.” Here we put it in the scene sequence to see tracking. Layers are split in Chapter 27.)
  5. Clear the programmer and raise only the tilt of the moving beams by one step. Store it as cue 1.2.
  6. Press GO in order from cue 1 and watch the stage (the 3D view).
How to check
  1. Open the sequence’s Track Sheet.
  2. If the wash dimmer and color cells of cues 1.1 and 1.2 are magenta, tracking is working correctly.
  3. Now leave values in the programmer, press Clear only twice, and store cue 2.
  4. If cue 2 is empty, you have seen the first row of Table 23-4 yourself.
  5. After checking, delete cue 2.
  6. This sequence is for practice, so delete it or move it to another number when you build the song page in Chapter 27 (Table 29-2, Chapter 29).
Try it with your own song
  1. Make the first three lines of the cue sheet of the song you are working on now into cues in the same way.
  2. For each cue, first write in one line what “changed,” and store only that.
  3. If you find it hard to write what changed in one line, that cue may be one that should be split in two.

Key points

  • Values pass through the programmer → presets and cues → sequences → executors. An executor is the fader and button that run a sequence.
  • Cues refer to presets. If you make presets first and make cues from presets, what you have to fix at rehearsal shrinks to one preset.
  • A cue stores only what changed, and the rest carries on by tracking. The magenta in the Track Sheet is the value carried over.
  • When priority is equal, the later value wins (LTP). To combine dimmers by taking the higher value, set the sequences’ priority to HTP. A playing sequence beats the programmer, so to hold the programmer’s values, turn on Freeze.
  • The places you get stuck first are Clear three times, clearing after storing, and priority.
On the job tomorrow
  1. Look once at what is in the programmer before you store, and always clear it after you store.
  2. This one habit prevents most cases of strange values secretly mixing into the next cue.
