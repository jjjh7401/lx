Chapter 27

Programming a Song into Cues

The song page and layer sequences

In this chapter
  • Move the Chapter 16 cue sheet onto one song page. The scene is one sequence, and each rhythm layer is one sequence of its own.
  • When you return fixtures that were running an effect to a fixed value, prevent the old effect from lingering and still moving.
  • Leave the moment a cue passes, and the preparation in between, to the console, with triggers and MIB.
Opening scene

Picture the metronome on a desk. Grab the weight of a wound-up metronome with your finger and the ticking stops. But let go and the weight swings again. Holding the weight only stopped this instant; the force wound into the spring is still there. To really stop it, you have to unwind the spring or set the stop.

The same thing happens on a console. In the chorus the movers sway in a pan wave, and then at the kick-out bar you go to a cue that turns off every layer and stops the movers in one place. The cue sheet clearly says “stop,” yet on stage the movers keep turning. The stop cue grabbed only the first step of the phaser, and the other steps were still there like the wound spring.

This chapter covers the process of programming a cue sheet into console cues. First you make the song page and divide the layers into sequences. Then you make the places where an effect hands over to a fixed value safe, and set the moment a cue passes with triggers and MIB.

27.1 One Page per Song

Put one song on one executor page, and make every song’s page with the same layout. Using the same page layout for the music-show of Chapter 17 looks like this on the console.

Table 27-1 Executors of the song page Starting value
Slot Sequence What it holds
1 Scene The whole Chapter 16 cue sheet (from the 0.5 blocking to the 99 finish)
2–7 Rhythm layers Back low bass (808), floor beam kick, side hi-hat, pixel claps, upper moving, lower moving
8 Accent Brightness hit, moving burst, red heart, white flash
9 Key All key fixtures (outside the song sequences, Auto Fix)
10 Safety Safety look, dark cue, black, freeze (outside the song sequences, Auto Fix)

Even if the layer names and number differ a little from song to song, do not change the slots. Leave unused layers as empty cells. Once your hands have memorized the slots, you press “the back is the second cell” without searching, even on a song page you have never seen.

A few things in the sequence settings should be set to fit the song page.1

  • Restart ModeDecides whether, when the sequence is switched on again, it goes to the first cue (First Cue) or the current cue (Current Cue) or the next cue (Next Cue). Set the scene sequence to First Cue and a restart of the song begins from the blocking cue.
  • Off when OverriddenWhen other sequences override all the values this sequence was sending, this sequence is switched off automatically.
  • Auto Start, Auto StopRaise the fader and it switches on; lower it to 0 and it switches off.
  • Auto FixKeeps that executor in its place even when you change pages. Use it for Key and Safety.

27.2 Rhythm Layer Sequences

Keep the rhythm layers out of the scene sequence, with a separate sequence for each layer. You are moving the sequence list divided by role in Chapter 13 onto the console as it is.

Put the effects in the scene cues together and three things become awkward. “Let’s take out just the back” becomes a job of editing cues; it is hard to change the speed of each layer separately; and every time you pass a scene cue the effects restart or linger.2 Keep layer sequences simple. Put the phaser presets made in Chapter 25 in cue 1 and switch them on and off with the executor. For a layer whose shape changes by section, like the back low bass, put a different phaser preset in cue 2 and move to that cue to match the scene cue.

When to switch a layer on and off is written in the “Layers” cell of the Chapter 16 cue sheet. There are three ways to move this onto the console.

  • By handSwitch the layer executors on and off yourself along with the scene cue’s GO. Suited to songs with few layers and to busking (Chapter 21).
  • With the scene cue’s Command fieldEach cue has a Command field in which you write a command-line command; Command Enabled next to it turns it on and off, and Command Delay can delay it.3 Write the commands that switch on the back low bass and the pixel claps in the Command field of cue 30, and a single Go of cue 30 turns the layers on together. For example, if the back low bass is sequence 12 (slot 2 of the song page), write Go+ Sequence 12 (send sequence 12 to its next cue; a sequence that was off is switched on by this), and to turn it off, Off Sequence 12 (switch sequence 12 off). Check once that your console accepts this syntax as it is.[Author to confirm]
  • With timecodeGive each layer a timecode track and record the times to switch it on and off (Chapter 28).

Whichever way, the principle of Section 13.5 stands. The scene takes color, position and the base dimmer; the layers take only dimmer pulses or relative-value positions. Divide color and position so two sequences do not touch them together.

There are, though, places where the scene’s base and a layer’s pulse must share the same attribute, like the back’s dimmer. Then you decide with priority which one comes out (Section 23.4).4

  • Layer on HighWhile the layer is on, the layer’s pulse (100% and 0%) overrides the scene’s base, and when you turn the layer off it returns to the scene’s base. The stage drops to 0 on the pulse’s off step, so the hit is sharp.
  • Scene and layer both HTPThe higher of the scene’s base and the layer’s pulse comes out. If only the layer is set to HTP, HTP has higher priority than LTP, so the layer’s value comes out as it is. It does not fall below the base even on the pulse’s off step, so the pulse sits on top of the base.

This book’s exercises use High. The manual does not state the default priority, so look at it in the settings window each time you make a new sequence.[Author to confirm]

27.3 When Going from an Effect to a Fixed Value

When you return fixtures that were running an effect to a fixed value, fill several steps of the fixed preset with the same value, and use that preset in the cue that turns the effect off too. Match the number of steps to the effect with the most steps in the show.

In terms of the metronome in the opening scene, this is the way of unwinding the spring. The principle combines the tracking of Chapter 23 with the steps of Chapter 25. A one-step value stored in a cue overwrites only step 1. The values from step 2 on left by the earlier cue are not told to change, so they remain by tracking, and the phaser keeps moving between step 1 and the remaining step. In one of the author’s shows the movers kept swaying in most of the cues that should have stopped, and after he filled the fixed presets up to the show’s maximum number of steps, all of them stopped.5 The manual does not separately describe how phaser steps track into the next cue, so this explanation is based on what the author observed.

Before the fix / After the fix
  • Before the fixOnly the “Downlight” position preset (step 1) was put into kick-out cue 39, which follows the chorus’s upper moving (two-step pan wave). In cue 39 the movers keep swaying near the downlight.
  • After the fixFill “Downlight” with the same value in all three steps and store it again. The phaser with the most steps in this show file is the 3-step one (the comet tail of Table 25-3), so every fixed preset is filled to 3 steps. The cue that turns off the upper moving layer uses the same preset.

The console also has a Stomp function that stops a phaser. It stops a running phaser, puts that attribute’s last output value into step 1, and throws away the other steps.6 If, with the programmer on step 1, you call a fixed preset on an attribute where a phaser is running, Stomp is applied automatically.6 But that is for working in the programmer. Auto Stomp in the sequence settings makes a cue’s fixed value stop a phaser running in another playback (another sequence or executor).1 It is hard to say that it also cleans up steps left by tracking inside the same sequence. There is also no confirmed source on whether a cue’s Release field (a field that, set to Yes, releases tracked values at that cue) or /CueOnly cleans up leftover steps.[Author to confirm] So this book uses filling the steps as the basic method.

How many steps remain in a stored cue is hard to read straight from a table on screen.5 For each fixed cue, look with your eyes in the 3D view or on stage to see whether it has stopped.

27.4 Triggers and MIB

Set the moment of going to the next cue with a trigger, and leave moving fixtures ahead of time while it is dark to the console with MIB (Move In Black).

Table 27-2 Cue triggers (Trig Type) 3
Trigger When the cue passes In LOVE ATTACK
Go When a command such as Go+ (a command to go to the next cue, the same as the Go button) arrives (by hand or timecode) Most cues
Time When the set time has passed since the previous cue started Cue 80 white flash → 80.1, one beat later (0.54 s, Calculation)
Follow When the previous cue’s transition has completely finished The scene right after a fade ends
Sound When sound comes in —
BPM On the beat of the sound input —

Timecode is not among the trigger types. Timecode is what sends Go to a cue (Chapter 28), and the cue’s trigger stays at Go. Trig Time is used only when the trigger is Time or Follow.3

You usually change a trigger by pressing the Trig Type cell of the Sequence Sheet. To change it from the command line, treat the trigger as one property of the cue and use a command that sets that property’s value. In this book’s numbering rules, song 1 (LOVE ATTACK)’s sequences start at 11, and the scene sequence is 11 (Table 29-2 in Chapter 29). The command to change the trigger of cue 80.1 in this scene sequence to Time is as follows.

Set Cue 80.1 Sequence 11 Property 'TrigType' 'Time'

Read it from the front like this.

  • SetThe command to set a value.
  • Cue 80.1 Sequence 11Cue 80.1 in sequence 11.
  • Property 'TrigType'Of that cue’s properties, the trigger type (the Trig Type field).
  • 'Time'The value to change to. To change to another trigger, put a name from Table 27-2 here (the one the author confirmed on the console is Time).

Together it means “in sequence 11’s cue 80.1, set the trigger type to Time.” The author confirmed on onPC 2.4.2 that the trigger changes in this form.7 At first, change it by pressing the cell in the Sequence Sheet, and use the command line when you repeat the same job over many cues.

MIB is a function in which the tracking sequence looks ahead to the next cue and moves the position and color of fixtures whose dimmer is 0 before they can be seen.8 The “meanwhile to the pan position and aqua” written in the dark-cue row of the Chapter 16 cue sheet is exactly what MIB does.

Table 27-3 MIB modes 8
Mode When it moves
Default Follows the MIB mode in the sequence settings
None No MIB in this cue
Defined In the cue written in the MIB Target field
Early As soon as the dimmer closes
UponGo When going on to the next cue after the dimmer has closed (one cue later than Early)
Late In the cue just before the dimmer opens again

LOVE ATTACK’s dark cue 29 is only two beats (1.07 s, Calculation; 59 is one beat, 79 is three), so Early, which moves as soon as the dimmer closes in cue 29, fits. Conversely, in a place where a mover’s motor noise could be heard, like a quiet scene in a ballad, do no MIB (None) or fix the place to move to one cue (Defined). MIB Preference (0–100) is the score MIB uses when choosing a cue; 0 is Never and the default is Normal 50.8 “Never” is the name of this score, not a mode, so to turn MIB off by mode, choose None.

The 2.5 Sequence Sheet has six fields about MIB. Besides MIB Mode, MIB Preference and MIB Target, there are MIB MultiStep, which decides what to do with phasers during MIB, and MIB Fade and MIB Delay, which set the time of the move.3 If movers that were swaying during the dark cue do not move to the next scene’s position, look at MIB MultiStep first.[Author to confirm]

For MIB to work, the fixture’s dimmer must really be 0 in the dark cue, and the position to move to must be stored in the next cue. Among dimmer values, Hold (a special value you can put in the dimmer field instead of 0) looks like 0% on stage but does not trigger MIB. Use it when you want to keep fixtures from moving on purpose while it is dark.8

27.5 The Habit of Storing Cues

When you store a cue, make and store only what changed, from a cleared programmer, and clear it again after storing. It is the habit from Chapter 23 repeated for the 32 cues of one song.

If you store again onto an existing cue, a window opens to choose a store option among Overwrite, Merge, Remove and Release.9

  • MergeAdds the current values to that cue. Use it for small fixes to a cue.
  • OverwriteEmpties that cue and writes it fresh with the current values.
  • RemoveTakes the attributes you have selected out of that cue.
  • ReleasePuts in a value that releases the tracked value in that cue.

A cue part holding a phaser has a Sync setting. It makes the phaser’s timing and phase start aligned as set when the cue is called.3 If a layer sequence starts out of step with the song’s beat, look at this setting and the Learn of Chapter 28. From 2.5, in the Sequence Sheet, Sync is gathered under a new Phaser column together with Speed Master, Speed Scale, Morph and Delay To Phase.11 For cue-part settings about phasers you only need to look in this one column. Morph makes fixtures move to the new phase without missing a beat when the cue changes. Delay To Phase moves the delay time given per attribute to the time a multi-step phaser starts. Speed Master and Speed Scale are the speed master and scale that the cue part’s phaser follows (Chapter 28).3

Some show file templates have a “capture” macro that gathers output made by busking into cues in one go. That is a feature of that template and not something grandMA3 has by default.10 The exercises in this book use only the default features.

27.6 [Exercise] Build LOVE ATTACK’s Song Page

In the show file you made in Chapters 24–25, build LOVE ATTACK’s song page. The cue sheet of Table 16-4 in Chapter 16 is the blueprint.

Follow along

  1. Clear executor page 2 (song 1’s page under Chapter 29’s numbering rules), and in slots 1–8 of Table 27-1 make the song’s eight sequences (sequences 11–18). Make Key and Safety in slots 9 and 10 as sequences shared by all songs (among 1–10) and turn Auto Fix on.
  2. Store the cue sheet’s 32 cues in the scene sequence with their numbers as they are (0.5, 1, 1.1, …, 99). Reflect the two fixes from Section 16.5 (cues 20 and 50 get a one-beat fade; Choruses 1 and 2 use the narrow Rooftop fan and Chorus 3 the wide one). In each cue put only what changed in the “Scene” field, and put color and position in with the presets of Chapter 24.
  3. Put the Chapter 25 phaser presets into the six rhythm layers. If a layer has two phasers (back low bass, floor beam kick, upper moving), put them in cues 1 and 2 in the order used. Set the priority of the layer sequences to High.
  4. Fill the fixed position presets (Intro, Downlight, Wall, Rooftop fan narrow, Rooftop fan wide, Cross beam, Sky beam) with the same value up to this show’s maximum number of steps (3) and store them again.
  5. Give dark cues 29 and 59 and breaks 79, kick-outs 39 and 69 MIB Early. For cue 80.1, change the trigger to Time and set Trig Time to 0.54 s.
  6. Choose one of the ways to switch layers on and off (by hand, the Command field, timecode). In this chapter do it by hand, and move to timecode in Chapter 28.
How to check
  1. Play the audio each of you has prepared, press GO at the times on the cue sheet, and go to the end.
  2. Look at three places separately.
  3. Whether the upper and lower moving really stop at cues 39 and 69 (kick-outs); whether the beam movers that went dark during the two beats of cue 29 move to the pan position and come on without any movement at cue 30; and whether at cue 90 it goes down to Pearl Heart · night over two bars.
  4. If movers sway where they should stop, look at the step count of Section 27.3.
Try it with your own song
  1. Build the same song page from your own song’s cue sheet.
  2. First match the layer cells to the order of the slots on the song page, then count the largest number of steps among the phasers used in the show and write it on the fixed presets.

Key points

  • One song goes on one executor page, and every song keeps the same layout. Key and Safety are fixed with Auto Fix.
  • The scene is one sequence, and each rhythm layer is one sequence. Divide the attributes so two sequences do not touch the same one.
  • When going from an effect to a fixed value, fill the fixed preset with the same value up to the show’s maximum number of steps, and use that preset in the cue that turns it off too.
  • The triggers are Go, Time, Follow, Sound and BPM, and timecode is not a trigger.
  • MIB moves the next scene’s position and color during a dark cue. Use Early for short dark cues, and None or Defined for quiet scenes.
On the job tomorrow
  1. When you run the cues at rehearsal, carry a separate list of “cues that must stop,” and check once for each of those cues that the movers really did stop.
