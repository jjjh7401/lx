Chapter 24

Patch, Groups and Presets

Moving the rig into the console

In this chapter
  • Move the Chapter 22 patch sheet (ID, address, coordinates) and its three levels of groups into the patch and the group pool as they are.
  • Learn that a group stores not values but the selection and order of fixtures, and that this order becomes the direction of an effect.
  • Store presets as Selective, and prevent the accidents where only a few fixtures change or the phase disappears.
Opening scene

In your phone’s gallery, pick ten of twenty photos and put a black-and-white filter on them all at once. Looking later, only the ten you picked are black and white and the rest are unchanged. Of course. You applied the filter only to the photos you picked.

Presets on a lighting console are the same. If you picked only a few fixtures when you stored a color preset, then when you call that preset later only those few change. The complaint heard on site, “I called the preset and only a few fixtures changed color,” is exactly this case. The preset is not broken; what you picked at the time of storing has simply stayed with it.

This chapter covers the three steps of moving the rig into the console: patch, groups and presets. In all three, “what you picked” and “in what order you picked it” decide the result.

24.1 Patch: Fixture Numbers and Addresses

Patching is giving each fixture a number (fixture ID) and a DMX address. You can carry the numbers, addresses and coordinates of the Chapter 22 patch sheet over from the top as they are.

On grandMA3 the basic number for calling a fixture is the FID (Fixture ID), and on the command line you write it like Fixture 201. You must have this number to select and control a fixture.1 You enter the address in the form universe.address. Universe 2, channel 1 is 2.1.

Numbering the Chapter 6 practice rig (96 fixtures) by the Chapter 22 rules gives Table 24-1. The addresses follow the universe plan of Table 22-10 in Chapter 22. Each type gets a block of numbers in the hundreds, and within the same type the numbers run from the left as seen from the audience (stage right) to the right (stage left).

From 2.5 the window for adding fixtures (Insert New Fixture) and the window for importing fixture types (Fixture Type Import) have a channel map. It shows in what order the channels of each mode of a fixture type are laid out, so even two modes with the same channel count can be told apart by their structure. There are two views, Channels (default) and Structure.12 Before patching, check here that the channel count and mode settled in Chapter 22 are right.

The manual sets no rule for ID number ranges.1 So a person decides them show by show, and the rule you settled goes into a show file template (Chapter 29) to use again next show.

24.2 Direction and Coordinates

If a fixture really moves the wrong way, do not patch it up with a preset; flip the direction in the patch (invert). If you patch it up with a preset, every move you make afterwards gets tangled together with it.2

grandMA3’s patch has three kinds of pan and tilt invert fields, and they do different things.1

Table 24-2 The three inverts in the patch 1
Field What changes When to use
DMX Invert Pan/Tilt The DMX value sent to the fixture When the 3D view and external visualizers are right but the real fixture moves the opposite way
Invert 3D Pan/Tilt The 3D view only (DMX stays the same) When the real fixture is right but only the 3D view is reversed
Enc Invert Pan/Tilt Only the direction the encoder turns When you want one encoder to move two facing rows (hung fixtures and upright fixtures) the same way

You enter a fixture’s place as 3D coordinates. The units are meters and degrees, and a newly added fixture sits at the origin (0, 0, 0).3 The axes are as follows.

  • XLeft and right of the stage. +X is stage left (the right as seen from the audience). If you set the center of the stage to 0, stage right is negative.4
  • YFront to back. +Y is upstage.
  • ZHeight. A fixture’s reference point is usually its hanging point, so if a fixture appears under the floor, raise Z to the hanging height.4

The coordinates you enter in the patch become the fixture’s place in the simulation as they are. Fixture layouts made in a drawing program or simulation program are exchanged as MVR files.13 That way the layouts of the drawing, the console and the simulation can be matched without re-entering coordinates one by one.

There is a trap when you enter negative numbers. On the command line, if there is a space after -, it becomes a relative value. Attribute "Pan" At - 10 means reduce the pan by 10, not set the pan to −10.5 The console puts a space between - and the number automatically, so to enter an absolute negative value, delete the space or use the +/- key of the calculator.[Author to confirm]

Words to Know
Stage left
The left as seen by a performer on stage looking at the audience, and the right as seen from the audience. The pictures in this book are seen from the audience, so the right of a picture is grandMA3’s +X.
Left and right in group names
When you write “left” or “right,” also write which side it is measured from. Writing “stage right,” as for the sides in Table 24-1, avoids confusion.

24.3 Groups and Selection Order

A group holds no values. A group stores only which fixtures you picked, in what order, and in what grid.6 This order and grid are used when you enter range values or build a phaser (Chapter 25).

When you store a group, the order in which you picked the fixtures becomes the order of the Selection Grid as it is, because a newly picked fixture is attached to the next empty cell along the grid’s X axis. The setting that decides this behavior is Grid Merge Mode, and its default, Append X, means “attach at the next cell on the X axis.”6 So a group stored after picking the 8 moving spots from 301 to 308 and a group stored after picking them from 308 to 301 are different groups, even though they are the same 8 fixtures. Apply the same wave effect and one runs from left to right and the other from right to left. The author saw with his own eyes on onPC 2.4.2 that reversing the selection order made the wave run backwards too.7

The three levels of groups from Chapter 22 are used here. All groups and sub-groups are groups that pick places, and effect groups are groups that pick an order. “Spots left → right,” “Spots right → left,” “Beams outside → in” and “Beams odd/even” are groups with the same fixtures but a different order.

Table 24-3 Group pool numbers Starting value
No. Level Groups
1–10 All groups 1 Key, 2 Fill, 3 Wash, 4 Back, 5 Side, 6 Spot, 7 Beam, 8 Pixel (LED bar main fixtures), 9 Strobe, 10 Blinder
11–30 Sub-groups Side stage right · left, Beam floor · tower, Beam stage right · left, all Pixel sub-fixtures
31–60 Effect groups Spot left → right · right → left · outside → in, Beam odd/even · outside → in · left → right, Side cross left/right, Pixel sub-fixtures left → right · center → out

After storing a group, check the order with your eyes. Turn on Highlight and step through with Next, and the fixtures light one at a time in the group’s order.8 If you skip this check, you get the case where an effect “was stored fine but the shape is strange.”

24.4 Keep Main Fixtures and Sub-fixtures in Different Groups

For fixtures with several sub-fixtures, like an LED bar, make a main fixture group and a sub-fixture group separately. If you mix them in one group, a phaser counts the fixtures in the wrong unit.

If you select fixtures with several sub-fixtures and press the Down key, the sub-fixtures spread out onto the grid in the order of their layout inside the fixture (Up goes back to the previous selection).8 If you apply a phaser without spreading them, the console sees one fixture with several sub-fixtures as one fixture. If you want a chase that moves differently for each sub-fixture, you must apply it to a sub-fixture group spread out with Down.[Author to confirm]

Also divide the attributes that main fixtures and sub-fixtures take. Main fixtures take attributes that apply to the whole fixture, like tilt, strobe and zoom, and sub-fixtures take brightness and color. And if you leave the main fixture’s dimmer at 100% and let the sub-fixtures take the brightness, the pixel effect is not hidden by the main fixture’s dimmer.[Author to confirm]

Some fixtures have two dimmers. The author once had a fixture that shines only when both the dimmer and the second dimmer (Dimmer2) are 100, and a dimmer preset stored mixed with another group left out the second dimmer, so no light came out.7 When you get a new fixture type, first look at how many dimmer channels it has.

24.5 Preset Pools

Presets are divided into a separate pool for each type (Feature Group), and those that mix several types go in the All pools.

A new show has 9 preset pools named for the basic types (Dimmer, Position, Color and so on) and 5 All pools with no input filter (All 1 to All 5). A pool for one type accepts only the attributes of that type.9 The manual has no table of pool numbers, but in the author’s show the Dimmer pool was number 1, the Position pool 2, the Color pool 4 and the All 1 pool 21.7 Pool numbers may differ from show to show, so look at the pool list once before you store.

Pool Presets Earlier chapter
Dimmer 0 (black), 25 (base), 50, 75, 100 (full) The Chapter 16 cue sheet
Color Pearl Pink, Lilac, Aqua, Red (special), White The Chapter 8 palette
Position Intro (narrow toward the floor), Downlight, Wall, Rooftop Fan narrow (Chorus 1 · 2), Rooftop Fan wide (Chorus 3), Cross Beam, Sky Beam The Chapter 9 look sheet, Sections 16.4 · 16.5
Beam Narrow (beam), wide (wash), prism The Chapter 6 attributes
All Phasers that mix two or more attributes (e.g., a pixel clap where color and dimmer run together) Chapter 25

For dimmers, make only the reference brightness steps among the levels of the cue sheet. In LOVE ATTACK’s cue sheet (Table 16-4 in Chapter 16), 0 appears in the main role of blocking and dark cues, 25 in the base of Pre-chorus 1, 50 in the intro and Verse 1, 75 in the base of Pre-chorus 2 and Chorus 3, and 100 in Chorus 3. If you keep these steps as presets, then when someone says at rehearsal “the base is too bright,” you fix just the base 25 preset and every cue that uses it follows (Chapter 23). Conversely, a brightness used in only one cue (45% in the bridge, 85% in the beat section and so on) may go into the cue as a hard value. For fixtures with two dimmer channels (Section 24.4), put the second dimmer in the dimmer preset as well.

If you try storing a value holding both color and dimmer in the Color pool, it is not refused, but it is hard to tell on screen whether any attribute was filtered out.7 So when two or more types are mixed, put it in an All pool. In this book phasers are gathered in the All pools even if they use the attributes of only one type, so that the phasers to put into layer sequences can be found in one place (Chapter 25, Table 29-2 in Chapter 29).

24.6 Set the Preset Mode to Selective

There are three ways to store a preset (the preset mode), and in this book’s exercises everything is stored as Selective. Both the “only a few change” of the opening scene and the “phase disappears” we will meet in Chapter 25 arise from the preset mode.

Table 24-5 The three preset modes 10
Mode Fixtures it applies to Where the value comes from
Selective(S) Each fixture that had a value when stored Each fixture’s own value
Global(G) All fixtures of the same fixture type One per fixture type. If values differ within the same type, the fixture with the lowest ID gives the representative value and the rest are added as Selective
Universal(U) All fixtures that have the attribute The first active fixture at the time of storing

Selective applies only to the fixtures that had a value when stored.10 So, as in the opening scene, if you pick only a few fixtures and store, only those few change. The fix is simple. Before storing a preset, check that you have picked the whole group the preset will apply to.

Universal takes its value from the first active fixture at the time of storing.10 If you store a phaser whose phase differs from fixture to fixture as Universal, that difference disappears. The author stored LOVE ATTACK’s dimmer wave as Universal and saw fixtures of the same type blink all at once. In another show, a color preset whose preset mode had not been set was stored as Universal, and most of the show’s colors came out different from the design.7 Since then he always stores presets with /Selective.

Each pool has a default preset mode, shown by a letter at the top right of the pool’s title. Either change Preset Mode to Selective in the pool settings, or pick it in Store Options, which appears when you hold Store down while storing. On the command line you attach the option, as in Store Preset 4.1 "Pearl Pink" /Selective.10 [Author to confirm] Reading from the front: Store is the command to store, Preset 4.1 is cell 1 of preset pool 4 (Color), “Pearl Pink” is the name to attach, and /Selective is the option that sets the preset mode to Selective. Check the pool number show by show, as in Section 24.5 above.

Words to Know
Phase
How far apart you offset the starting point of each fixture within the same phaser. You give it in degrees (0–360°). Chapters 25 and 26 cover it in detail.

24.7 The 3D View and Simulation Programs

When you work without a real stage, you see the result on a simulation screen. The exercises in this book use the 3D view built into grandMA3 onPC. If a beam does not show, look first not at the fixture but at the 3D view’s settings or selection.

Table 24-6 What to look at when a beam does not show in the 3D view 11 7 12
What to look at Why
The graphics of the PC running onPC From 2.5 the 3D view draws with Vulkan instead of OpenGL. If the 3D window is blank or very slow, look first at the graphics driver and the system requirements of 2.5 12
Whether Stage in the 3D window settings is “All stages” If the patch has several stages, the 3D window can show only one. If imported structures and lights are split across different stages, the lights do not show
Beam fader, Haze The 3D window adjusts separately how visible the beam is and the beam shafts in the air
Fixtures with two dimmers If the second dimmer is 0, no light comes out
Fixtures with sub-fixtures In the author’s case, the beam showed in 3D only when the main fixture and sub-fixtures were selected together (a dot at the end of the number) and tilt was given too
The fixture’s brightness A fixture type with low light output is drawn much darker in 3D even at 100%

On site, besides the console’s 3D view, external simulation programs are also used. An external program receives the console’s signal and draws the fixtures, and shows the stage and even the LED wall more realistically. You can also turn cues into video to show the director or the artist.14 15

Examples The 3D view built into onPC and the console Capture, Depence, WYSIWYG and others

Either way, a simulation’s beams are not the real stage. Values settled in the simulation are checked by running the whole song (Section 28.5), and only the places that change on the real stage are looked at again at rehearsal (Chapter 30).

24.8 [Exercise] Patch the Practice Rig and Make LOVE ATTACK’s Basic Presets

Patch the Chapter 6 practice rig in onPC, and build the three levels of groups and LOVE ATTACK’s basic presets. You will keep using this show file through Chapter 27.

Follow along

  1. Following the patch sheet from Chapter 22 (Table 22-10, Web tool 18), patch the 94 fixtures with numbers and addresses (the 2 follow spots are run by a person, off the console, so they are not patched). You may choose similar fixture types; for the mode, pick by the channel map the one closest to the channel counts of Table 22-11 in Chapter 22.
  2. Place the fixtures according to the Chapter 22 lighting plot (Figures 22-3 and 22-4, Table 22-8) and the coordinates of the patch sheet. The origin is the center of the front edge of the stage, and fixtures on stage right have negative X.
  3. Make the all-groups and sub-groups with the numbers of Table 24-3, and pick the effect groups while thinking about the order. For the LED bars, spread the sub-fixtures with Down and make the sub-fixture groups separately.
  4. Change the Preset Mode of the Dimmer, Color and Position pools to Selective.
  5. Make the presets of Table 24-4. For dimmers, select all the fixtures and store each step; for fixtures with two dimmers, enter the same value in the second dimmer too. For colors, use the RGB values of Chapter 8, and before storing, check that you picked the whole group that will use the color.
  6. For positions, match the pictures of the Chapter 9 look sheet in the 3D view, then store.
How to check
  1. Look at three things.
  2. First, for each effect group, step through the order with Highlight and Next and see that it moves like Figure 24-1.
  3. Second, in the preset pools, see that the preset mode letter of every preset is S.
  4. Third, select all fixtures and call Pearl Pink, and see that every one of the 94 fixtures that has color changes.
  5. If some are left over, first check whether those fixtures were picked at the time of storing.
Try it with your own stage
  1. Fill in Tables 24-1 and 24-3 with the fixtures of the show you are working on now.

Key points

  • Patching is giving each fixture an ID and an address, and IDs are given by type in number blocks of hundreds. The manual has no fixed rule, so settle one show by show and keep it as a template.
  • If the direction is wrong, invert it in the patch, not in a preset. There are three inverts: DMX, 3D and encoder.
  • +X is stage left and stage right is negative. On the command line, - 10 (with a space) is a relative value.
  • A group stores the selection, order and grid. The order you picked in is the direction of the effect.
  • For fixtures with sub-fixtures, make the main fixtures and sub-fixtures different groups.
  • Store presets as Selective. At the time of storing, check that you picked the whole group they will apply to.
On the job tomorrow
  1. Before storing a preset, check only two things: that you have picked the whole group that will use it, and that the pool’s preset mode letter is S.
