Lighting Design Tools

The hands-on tools of the book, ready to use in the field. The three tools of Part 1 let you listen to a song and gather it into a song map. The four tools of Part 2 design its concept, colors, looks and brightness. The four tools of Part 3 place those on beats and bars and carry them into a cue sheet. The four tools of Part 4 fit the result to the show type (broadcast, concert, festival) and the rig. The three tools of Part 5 work out the numbers, phasers and timecode of a grandMA3 show file, and the three tools of Part 6 check the rehearsal, practice GO and confirm the safety limits. All tools share the same song notes, so what you write in one carries straight into the next.

The 153-effect dictionary, working templates and reference tables are collected in the Resource library ↗ (Korean for now).

You open your own music files. A file plays inside this browser only and is never uploaded anywhere. All that is saved is your short notes, and they stay in this browser only.

Part 1 · Reading the Song

Step 1 · Ch. 1

Listening Notes

Open a music file, play it, and mark each change on its bar with a single key. On the third pass, pick a color for each section.

In the field: the day you get a new song, before you switch on the consoleOpen → Step 2 · Ch. 1

Sound and Light Matching

For each section, choose which of the sounds you hear the light should follow. If it follows too many, the tool flags stage clutter.

In the field: when you are out of cue ideas, when the chorus looks noisyOpen → Step 3 · Ch. 5

Song Map

Gathers your notes into a basic and a detailed song map, and points out the end time of the music, how many sections are at energy 5, and what to listen for before rehearsal.

In the field: when sharing with the team, a last check before rehearsalOpen →

Part 2 · Designing the Light

Ch. 7 · 10

Concept Sheet

Write the concept layers you read from the song, a one-line concept, the lights you will not use, motifs, scene color chips and questions for the meeting, and gather them with palette, looks and brightness on one sheet.

In the field: the day before the concept meetingOpen → Ch. 8

Palette

Define colors by name and RGB value and choose two colors per section. It also compares pixel-bar color mixes.

In the field: before you record color presetsOpen → Ch. 9

Look Sheet

For each section, write a look name, one main, positions, effects and the in time.

In the field: before building cues, for rehearsal notesOpen → Ch. 10

Brightness Plan

Set the brightness of each section and draw the curve, then write the bar where the first dark cue and special cue appear.

In the field: when every cue looks like 100%Open →

Part 3 · Directing the Song

Ch. 11 · 13

Beat Grid

Write what each role does in lines and four-bar cells, and mark the rhythm layer (♪) and the main (★). Follow the current cell by clicking along or with your own audio.

In the field: before you record cues, when the stage looks busyOpen → Ch. 12

Section Guide

Shows a starting value from the section, energy and BPM, and asks you to write a reason in any cell where your design differs.

In the field: with a song you just received, when checking a designOpen → Ch. 14

Transition Curves

Compares the brightness curves of the two transition tools in beats and seconds, and calculates the in time at each boundary of the song.

In the field: when deciding in timesOpen → Ch. 16

Cue Sheet

Builds a first cue sheet draft from your notes and runs fourteen self-checks to help you choose keep, fix or change.

In the field: the day before rehearsal, when handing over to the operatorOpen →

Part 4 · Lighting by Show Type

Ch. 17

Broadcast Check

For each flashing cue, enter the beat unit, area, length and red. It calculates Hz, compares it with the broadcast flash limit and goes through the camera checklist with you.

In the field: before the camera rehearsalOpen → Ch. 18 · 19

Setlist Arc

For each song, write the energy, main color, how it is run and the specials, then draw the arc and color band of the whole show. You can also build a run sheet for an awards show.

In the field: day one of designing a concert or awards showOpen → Ch. 18 · 19

Busking Page

Build a busking page from color, position, effect, accent and safety rows, and read the cycle in seconds with tap tempo.

In the field: on a stage where you do not know the setlistOpen → Ch. 21

Rig Calculator and Patch Sheet

Four steps: fixture list → numbers and addresses → positions and coordinates → patch sheet. It shows a universe map, a plot and checks, and lets you paste a spreadsheet or Vectorworks table or copy out the patch sheet.

In the field: when designing your own stage, when checking the patch of a stage you were givenOpen → [[5]][[6]]Ch. 22[[7]]

Part 5 · Building It on the Console — grandMA3

Part 6 · Rehearsal and Show

Ch. 28 · 30

Rehearsal Notes

Tick through the nine previs checks and ten rehearsal checks, write the problems you find with cue, bar and verdict, and get a to-do list in the order patch → preset → cue → timecode.

In the field: the day before rehearsal, during rehearsal, between rehearsalsOpen → Ch. 31

GO Practice

Plays a click at the song’s BPM, brings up the standby, and records how far ahead of or behind the cue’s beat you pressed GO.

In the field: before the show, after making the GO cue listOpen → Ch. 32

Safety Check

Compares flashing with broadcast and venue limits and gathers audience beams, haze and special-effects agreements, the weight and power of hung gear, and the show-notice deadline on one sheet.
