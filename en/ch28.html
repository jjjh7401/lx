Chapter 28

Tempo and Timecode

Matching the song’s speed and time

In this chapter
  • Put the song’s BPM on one speed master, and give each effect only a different Measure.
  • Learn that tap (Learn) sets the speed and the starting point together, and correct an effect that is half a beat off.
  • Record the actions on the song page onto a timecode track for each layer, and check them against the cue sheet.
Opening scene

Say you are clapping along to a friend’s song at karaoke. For the first few claps you wobble a little as you find the tempo, and soon you lock onto the beat. But sometimes the tempo is right and yet your claps keep landing half a beat late. You are clapping at the same speed as the song, but your clap lands not on “one” but on “one-and.” In that case the fix is not to clap faster; it is to stop clapping once and come back in on “one.”

A lighting effect is the same. Even when you have set the effect’s speed to the song’s BPM, the pulse can land half a beat behind the kick. Speed and the starting point are different problems.

In this chapter we tie the effects’ speed to one value and line up the starting point. Then we cover how to record the actions you have lined up on timecode and repeat them exactly every time.

28.1 The Song’s BPM and the Effect’s Measure

Put the song’s BPM on one speed master and have every phaser follow that master. The only thing that differs from effect to effect is Measure (the number of beats in one cycle).

In Chapter 25 we said Measure is “how many beats make one cycle.” If Speed is the speed of the beats, you only need to tie Speed to the song’s one BPM and give each effect a different Measure. The author sets speed master 15 to the song’s BPM (Master 3.15 At BPM 112) and gives every phaser At SpeedMaster 15 (connect the selected phasers’ speed to master 15). The reason for using 15 is that 16 is the special master that follows the sound input (Section 28.2), so the number just before it is kept for the song’s BPM alone. He confirmed on onPC that when the master’s BPM is changed, every effect follows on the spot.1 The 3 in the command means the speed master among the master types.2

Table 28-1 Length of one cycle (seconds) by BPM and Measure Calculation
BPM Measure 1 Measure 2 Measure 4 (1 bar) Measure 8 (2 bars)
80 0.75 1.50 3.00 6.00
100 0.60 1.20 2.40 4.80
112(LOVE ATTACK) 0.54 1.07 2.14 4.29
128 0.47 0.94 1.88 3.75
140 0.43 0.86 1.71 3.43

Tie them together like this and when the song changes you fix only the master’s one BPM. Conversely, if you had typed Speed into each effect directly, you would have to fix the effects one by one whenever the song’s BPM changed. Also avoid giving one attribute both At Speed and At SpeedMaster. The author has not measured what happens when the two are mixed, and there is no reason to mix them.1

When you have to read the song’s beat as half (half-time), do not change the master; use HalfSpeed. It halves the speed of a speed master or sequence, as in HalfSpeed Master 3.15.3 Reading songs over 160 BPM as half-time in Table 12-2 of Chapter 12 is this command.

28.2 Speed Masters and Tap

There are 16 speed masters, with values of 0–225 BPM. Master 16 is the master that automatically follows the sound input’s BPM.4

A speed master can be connected to a sequence, cue, cue part, attribute, preset or phaser.4 Choose a master in the Speed Master field of the sequence settings and the phasers inside that sequence follow it together.5 Make the connection in only one place, either on the phaser (attribute) or on the sequence. If the same master is connected to both the sequence and the attribute, the attribute’s connection is erased when you update or store.4 In 2.5, the field that connects a master in a preset was renamed Phaser Speedmaster and the scale field was renamed Phaser Scale. What the 2.4 sources call a preset’s Speed Master and Speed Scale are these two fields.14

2.5 has a new Masters window that shows the masters in one window (Add Window > More > Playback > Masters). There is a tab for each master type, and on the Speed tab each master has double (DS), half (HS) and Learn buttons. There are also buttons that return all speed masters to 60 BPM, or double or halve them all at once. A master with a scale applied gets an asterisk (*), and with Non-Default Only on, only masters that are not at their defaults show.14 When a song read as half-time ends, check in this window that no asterisk is left.

Master 16 looks convenient, but on site the detected BPM often jumps. Move this master’s fader and it settles only to the nearest multiple of the detected BPM.4 So for a song you know in advance, enter the BPM as a number, and for a song you hear for the first time (Chapter 21), match it with tap.

Tap is Learn on grandMA3. Hold the Learn | Rate1 key (the key on the console where Learn and Rate1 are printed together) and press an executor key several times at the speed you want, and it learns that speed.6 The Learn command combines LearnSpeed, which learns the speed, and ReSync, which realigns the starting point, so it sets the phaser’s speed and starting point together.7 This is the “stop clapping and come back in on one” of the opening scene. If the speed is right but it is half a beat late, do not tap faster; tap again to the kick’s “one.”

In Chapter 21 we said to tap to the kick, not the hi-hat. The manual does not say how many taps are needed, so start from Chapter 21’s starting value (four or more), try four and then eight on your own console, and get the number that settles into your hands.[Author to confirm] In 2.5 the speed master’s Learn Mode chooses how tap treats the scale (Speed Scale). The default is Learn Ignores Speed Scale, which ignores the scale; there are also Learn Respects Speed Scale, which follows the scale, and Auto Increase Speed Scale, which raises the scale automatically.4 When you tap on a master set to half-time the result depends on this setting, so look at your console’s Learn Mode once.[Author to confirm]

If you use master 16 or a sound trigger on onPC, look at one more thing. From 2.5 the audio input and output on onPC default to None, so you must choose an input device yourself to receive sound.14

Words to Know
Song Speed
Some show file templates name one speed master “Song Speed” and keep the song’s BPM there. It is not a grandMA3 feature name but a name that template gave. With default features, this chapter’s method (put the song’s BPM on one speed master and connect the phasers to that master) does the same job.
A short experiment
  1. Turn on your phone’s stopwatch, clap along to a favorite song, and count to 16.
  2. Stop the stopwatch at the 16th clap and calculate the BPM from the time since the first clap (BPM = 15 ÷ elapsed seconds × 60, Calculation: the span of 15 intervals). Measure the same song once to the kick and once to the hi-hat, and compare the two values.

28.3 A Timecode Track per Layer

Timecode is a way to record what you did by hand on the song page together with the song’s time, and repeat it exactly at every show. Use it on stages that play the same audio from the start every time, like the MR songs of Chapter 17 or the light show of Chapter 20.

grandMA3’s timecode is layered as timecode show → track group (a unit that bundles several tracks) → track, and one track takes care of one target (a sequence, preset, master and so on).8 9 The author gives each sequence on the song page its own track. One scene and six rhythm layers make seven tracks, and the cues go in as Go+ events.10

Recording is done like playing. Turn on Setup in the Timecode window and a Record button appears on the toolbar below. Press Record and play the song, and what you do on the executors (Go, switching on and off, even fader movement) goes into the related tracks automatically. The sequence you want to record must be assigned to an executor.8 You can fix the times of recorded events later.

Where does the time come from? Each timecode show has a place that receives time (TC Slot), and a new show starts out as Internal (the clock inside the console). An Internal timecode show can be run with the Go command.8 If you receive external timecode (timecode coming from the audio player), the audio and the lights run on the same clock. You can also give an Offset between the received time and the show’s time.11 In 2.5.0 there were problems in a session (a network joining several consoles) where timecode events were recorded overlapping, or remote events that were set not to record were recorded anyway. These were fixed in 2.5.1.0 and 2.5.1.1.15 If you build the show with timecode, use the bug-fix release and, after recording, count the events on each track (Section 28.5).

There is something to know when you rewind to check. The Timecode window’s toolbar has buttons for go to start, previous marker, stop, pause, play and record, and no separate rewind button. To get to where you want, press and hold the cursor and drag it, or move it with an encoder.11 If you put a marker at each section of the song, “previous marker” does the job of rewinding. And before a check playback, switch off every sequence that was on. If layers you switched on by hand are still there, you cannot tell whether the timecode is right or wrong.12

28.4 Choosing Between Timecode and Busking

If the lighting must hit the same moment of the audio every time, use timecode; if not, use busking by hand. A real show usually mixes the two.

Table 28-2 Timecode and busking 13
Timecode By hand (Go and busking on the song page)
Stages it suits MR songs, light shows, openings matched to video, tour shows that are the same every day Live bands, DJs, festivals where you do not know the setlist
Strengths The same cue on the same frame every time; the operator can watch other things Follows even if the tempo wobbles; fits that day’s audience
Be careful Record again if the audio changes; cannot follow a live tempo Misses cues that are too dense for hands to follow
How to mix Scene cues and rhythm layers on timecode, accents and key by hand Scene cues by hand, rhythm layers tied to BPM and automatic

In the music-show of Chapter 17 we said an MR song can be reproduced with timecode while a live band is followed by hand. Even the same song changes method when the stage changes. So the order is always the same. First finish the song page by hand, then record it on timecode. If you program with timecode a song you have never taken through by hand, then when something needs fixing it is hard to know where to fix it.

28.5 Running the Whole Song in a Simulation

Before rehearsal, play the music on the simulation screen, run the song from start to finish, and then check the values with the previz checklist.

When you make cues one at a time, you see only that cue. Only by running the whole song in a row do you see problems such as values carried over from the previous cue, effects that do not stop, and brightness that does not rise even when the chorus returns.

Words to Know
Previz
(Previz, Previsualization): Programming and checking the lighting by previewing it on a simulation screen before hanging the fixtures on the real stage.

How to run it is simple. If you recorded with timecode, play the timecode from the start; if you have not recorded yet, press GO by the cue sheet along with the audio. Record the whole simulation screen while you do this.

Look at the content, not the count. The console does not refuse to store a cue when a value is empty or wrong. So even when the number of cues, presets and timecode events matches the cue sheet, whether the values inside the cues are right must be checked separately.16 Open the Track Sheet (Chapter 23) and check, group by group, that the dimmer, color and position values are the same as the cue sheet says.17

Table 28-3 Previz checklist 16
No. What to check How to check Related chapter
1 Does every group that is on have a position? In the Track Sheet, whether cells that have a dimmer value also have a position value 9·24
2 Does every group that is on have a color? Check the color values the same way 8·24
3 Is there a group whose position never changes through the song? If so, compare with the cue sheet to see whether it was intended 9
4 Is any group left at the default position (the floor right under the fixture)? In the simulation screen, look for fixtures lighting the floor 9·24
5 Does the preset hold all the fixtures of that group? Whether the number of fixtures in the preset edit window equals the group’s quantity 24
6 Is every preset mode Selective? Whether the preset pools show the mode mark S 24
7 Played in order from the start, does it match the cue sheet? Whether values from the previous cue are left over and mixed in 16·23
8 Does the effect really stop in the cues where it should stop? Check the movers and dimmer at kick-outs, breaks and the end cue 27
9 Do the timecode tracks, event counts and times match the cue sheet? Count the events on each track and check the hit times 28

Items 1–4 are problems that arise when moving the cue sheet to the console, items 5–6 when storing presets, items 7–8 when playing back, and item 9 when recording timecode. Fix what is caught in the same order as rehearsal (patch → preset → cue → timecode) (Section 30.4).

Watch the recorded video together. Show the video to the director, the artist and the camera director, and you can get opinions right away on scenes that are hard to explain in the words of a cue sheet. Write the opinions you receive in the same format as the rehearsal notes (Chapter 30) and fix them before rehearsal. If you need more realistic video, use an external simulation program (Section 24.7).18 [Author to confirm]

But being right in the simulation does not mean being right on the real stage. Where the fixtures are hung, the real brightness and color, the haze, the cameras and the band’s tempo are looked at in rehearsal (Chapter 30).

28.6 [Exercise] Tie LOVE ATTACK to 112 BPM and Record It on Timecode

Tie the effects of the song page you built in Chapter 27 to one speed master, and record the scene and rhythm layers onto seven timecode tracks. Use the audio file each of you has prepared.

Follow along

  1. Set speed master 15 to 112 BPM (Master 3.15 At BPM 112).
  2. Check that all the phaser presets made in Chapter 25 follow master 15. Change the master from 112 to 100, and it is right if every layer slows down together. Leave the sequence settings’ Speed Master empty. If the same master is connected in both places, phaser and sequence, the phaser’s connection is erased when you store (Section 28.2).
  3. Load the Chapter 16 cue sheet into the Web tool 21 timecode table and pull out each track’s event times and count.
  4. Make a new timecode show and press Record in Setup. This exercise records with the console’s internal clock (Internal), so at the moment you press Record you also start the audio from the beginning. Because you are starting it by hand, it can be slightly off, and you fix that difference after recording with Offset or the event times. Press the scene GO and the layer on/off at the times on the cue sheet, as if playing.
  5. Stop recording and put a marker at each section (Intro, Verse 1, Pre-chorus 1, Chorus 1 …).
  6. Switch off every sequence that was on and play from the start.
  7. While it plays, record the whole 3D screen, and check the whole song with the previz checklist of Table 28-3. Mark the items that get caught in the previz checklist of Web tool 23.
How to check
  1. Compare three things.
  2. First, see whether the event count on each track equals the count you pulled in step 3.
  3. Second, see how much the event times differ from the cue sheet’s times.
  4. A hand-pressed recording is always a little late, so for events with a hit, like cue 30 (bar 18 beat 1, 0:37.9), fix the times to the cue sheet’s after recording.
  5. Third, deliberately put the BPM off with tap and realign to the kick’s “one” with Learn, and see whether the pulse that was half a beat late is corrected.
  6. Fourth, see whether all nine items of the step 7 previz checklist pass.
  7. If any are caught, fix them before rehearsal.
Try it with your own song
  1. Take your own song’s page through by hand to the end, then record it the same way.
  2. First mark the cues that were hard to press by hand (dense cues, cues that come in a beat early), record only those cues, and then fix the times.

Key points

  • Put the song’s BPM on one speed master; the only difference between effects is Measure. Read half-time with HalfSpeed.
  • There are 16 speed masters, 0–225 BPM, and master 16 follows the sound input. Detection can jump, so match a known song by number and a new song by tap. In 2.5 the Masters window shows all the masters at a glance.
  • Learn sets the speed and the starting point together. If it is half a beat late, tap again to the kick’s “one.”
  • Timecode is timecode show → track group → track, with one track per sequence. Record as if playing, and use markers instead of rewinding.
  • Finish the song page by hand, then record it on timecode.
  • Before rehearsal, run the whole song in the simulation, check the nine previz checklist items, and get the director’s and artist’s opinions from the recorded video. Look at the values inside the cues, not the number of cues.
On the job tomorrow
  1. Before starting rehearsal, write that day’s song BPMs on the speed masters and see that when the song changes you change only one number.
  2. If you are fixing the speed effect by effect, the tying is not done yet.
