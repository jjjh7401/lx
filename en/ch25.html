Chapter 25

Phaser Basics

The five settings of a moving effect

In this chapter
  • Learn that a phaser is made of two or more steps, and why a single step moves nothing.
  • Fix in a picture what each of the five parameters — Speed, Phase, Width, Transition and Measure — changes.
  • From a song’s BPM and Measure, calculate how many times per second it flashes, and build either a breathing pulse or a sharp-cut pulse.
Opening scene

A string of Christmas-tree lights usually has a single button on it. Each press changes the mode. In one mode the bulbs slowly brighten and dim, breathing; in another they flash in sharp ticks. In yet another the light runs along the string to one side.

Same bulbs, same speed, yet the feel is completely different. There are three differences: the shape in which the brightness changes (does it flow smoothly or cut?), the length of time a bulb is on, and how far each bulb’s starting point is offset.

The phaser that makes moving effects on grandMA3 also sets these three with numbers. Add the speed and the length of one cycle and you have five parameters. In Chapters 11–13 you described the rhythm layers in words — “the back once per beat,” “the sides alternating left and right.” This chapter is about moving those words into the five parameters.

25.1 A Phaser Starts at Two Steps

A phaser changes values by moving between two or more steps (slots, each holding one value to pass to in turn). A cue or preset with only one step does not move.1

For a dimmer pulse, put 100% in step 1 and 0% in step 2. For a color chase, put Pearl Pink in step 1 and Aqua in step 2. For a pan wave, put right in step 1 and left in step 2. The console moves between these steps at a set speed and in a set shape.

There are several ways to make steps.2

  • Hold Step and press presets one after anotherPress the white, red and blue presets in turn and you get a three-step color phaser.
  • MA + NextMakes a new step after the current one and moves to it. Put 0% in step 1, press MA + Next, then put in 100%, and you have a two-step dimmer phaser.
  • MA + SetSelects all steps at once. Use it when you give every step the same shape (Transition and so on).

Here comes the first trap. If you put Phase or Speed on a phaser that has only one step, the console does not refuse. The author met a case on onPC 2.4.2 where the console accepted every command and yet the stage stood still.3 When a phaser does not move, first check whether it has two or more steps.

Words to Know
A phaser’s “layers”
The grandMA3 manual calls values such as Speed, Phase and Width a phaser’s “layers.” In this book a layer means the rhythm layers of Chapter 13 (sequences such as the back’s low bass (808) or the side hi-hat), so to avoid confusion we call the phaser’s own values the “five parameters.”

25.2 The Five Parameters

A phaser’s shape is set by five parameters: Speed, Phase, Width, Transition and Measure. The five encoders on the Phaser Bar of the Phaser Editor are exactly these values.4

Table 25-1 The five parameters of a phaser 1 4 5
Parameter What it sets Unit and default In terms of the bulbs
Speed How fast the beats go BPM (or Hz, or seconds). Default 60 BPM How fast it flashes
Phase How far each fixture’s starting point is offset Degrees (0–360°) The direction and spacing in which the light flows
Width The length of one step % of one beat. 100% is one beat; default 100% How long it stays on
Transition The part of a step spent changing the value % of the step length. 0 means an instant change Flowing smoothly, or cutting
Measure How many beats make one cycle Number of beats (may be left empty) The time it takes to flash once and return to the start

On top of these five, Accel and Decel add to the shape. Accel is how the phaser leaves the current value and Decel is how it arrives at the next. 0% is constant (a straight line); −100% starts smoothly and arrives smoothly (like a sine curve).1 The Select Form buttons of the Phaser Editor (square, saw, sine, circle) are shortcuts that change Transition, Accel and Decel in one go.4

The five parameters have no value that sets size. How bright a dimmer pulse gets, or how wide a pan wave swings, is set by the difference between the values you put in the steps. Moving between 100% and 0% is large; between 60% and 40% it is small. The Size encoder of the Phaser Editor grows or shrinks all the steps at once around the center.4

25.3 Measure and the Flash Rate

How many times per second it flashes is set by the song’s BPM and Measure. For a phaser that lights once per cycle, one formula does the calculation.

One number

Flashes per second = BPM ÷ 60 ÷ Measure (Calculation). Put in the manual’s example (120 BPM, Measure 4 → repeats every 2 seconds) and you get 120 ÷ 60 ÷ 4 = 0.5, once every 2 seconds.1

Measure is “how many beats make one cycle.”1 So Measure 1 runs one cycle per beat, Measure 2 one every two beats, and Measure 4 one every bar. Setting the speed of an effect in beats rather than seconds, as we said in Chapter 2, is done on the console with Measure alone. Even if the song’s BPM changes, “once per bar” stays once per bar.

Table 25-2 Measure and flashing for LOVE ATTACK (112 BPM) Calculation
Measure One cycle Flashes per second (one fixture) Where it is used (Chapter 11 beat grid)
0.5 0.27 s 3.73 times Half-beat pulse (outside the broadcast limit, Section 17.5)
1 0.54 s 1.87 times Low bass on every beat
2 1.07 s 0.93 times Left-right alternation, a 2-beat pan wave
4 2.14 s 0.47 times First beat of the bar
8 4.29 s 0.23 times A 2-bar tilt wave

The formula has three conditions. If even one is off, the flash rate comes out double or half.

  • You must enter Measure. If you do not, one cycle is the sum of the step Widths. A two-step phaser with Width 100% has a cycle of 2 beats, so a 2 goes in the formula’s Measure slot.1
  • It must be a phaser that lights once per cycle. With four steps — on, off, on, off — it lights twice per cycle, so the count doubles too.
  • It is per fixture. In a six-way chase with different phases, each fixture flashes as the formula says, but across the whole stage the light bursts six times as often. When you check the broadcast flash limits of Chapters 10 and 17, look at the count and the area of the whole stage together.

The manual recommends making the sum of all steps’ Widths equal the number of beats of Measure. This is because, once Measure is entered, Measure sets the length of one cycle and Width is used only as the ratio that divides the length among the steps, so different Width combinations can give the same result.5 For example, at Measure 4, whether you give Width 25 and 75 or 100 and 300, step 1 is a quarter of the cycle, one beat.[Author to confirm]

The tables in this book (Tables 25-3 and 25-4, and Chapter 26) give Width as a percentage of one cycle taken as 100, to be easy to read. To enter them as the manual recommends, multiply each Width by Measure. Measure 4’s 25/75 becomes 100/300, and Measure 2’s 50/50 becomes 100/100 (Calculation).

25.4 Pulse Shapes: Sine and Snap

Even at the same speed, Transition and Accel·Decel can make a pulse soft as breathing or cut like a knife. This is the difference between the bulb modes in the opening scene.

  • Sine (breathing): two steps (0% and 100%), select all steps, Accel −100, Decel −100. The manual’s way of making a sine dimmer is this method, and the author also checked the sine curve by eye on onPC.6 Use it for the back on a slow song and for a wash breathing in a ballad.
  • Snap (on the beat): two steps (100% and 0%), Transition 0 on both. With Transition 0 the value changes instantly when the step starts and stays there for the rest of the time. The length it is on is set by the Width of step 1. The manual gives At Transition 0 + Width 50 (a command that puts Transition 0 and Width 50 in at once) as an example of a PWM effect (a square pulse that is half on and half off).5 Use it for hits such as a low-bass kick or a clap.
  • SawTransition 100 going from step 1 to 2, Transition 0 coming back from 2 to 1. It brightens slowly and then cuts off. Use it for the rising feel of a build-up.[Author to confirm]

If you give Transition to only some of the steps, the shape changes only halfway. Put the shape in after selecting all steps with MA + Set. Also, Accel·Decel must go in after all the steps are made. The author once put in Accel·Decel before making the steps, and the console accepted it but nothing happened.3

A short experiment
  1. On paper, draw a time axis across and a vertical tick for every beat.
  2. On one line draw a wave with one hill per beat (sine), and on the line below a square bar that is raised only for the first 30% of each beat (snap, Width 30).
  3. Play the song and follow the two lines with your finger.
  4. Feel which line fits the kick and which fits the pad.

25.5 Absolute and Relative Values, and Phase per Attribute

Make a position phaser with relative values and it moves in the same shape over any position. Laying the up-and-down moving sequence over a scene as relative values in Chapter 13 was this method.

A step can hold an absolute value (pan 30°) or a relative value (+10° from where it is now), and the console stores the two separately and adds them together.1 A circle made with absolute values always turns in the same place; a circle made with relative values turns around the current position. The author confirmed that a relative-value phaser, called over any position preset, gives the same shape around that position.3 So LOVE ATTACK’s pan wave is made with relative values, and the base position (Rooftop fan) is held by the scene sequence.

Phase can be given not only per fixture but also per attribute.1 So give pan and tilt a sine of the same size with the phase shifted 90° and you get a circle; give them 0° or 180° and you get a diagonal. When pan is farthest right, tilt is in the middle; when pan returns to the middle, tilt is at its highest; when pan is farthest left, tilt is in the middle again — the two movements offset by a quarter cycle each, and the end of the beam draws a circle.[Author to confirm] The manual describes another way to make a circle (two absolute points and a handle), so try both and pick the one you find easier.7

Position phasers start slowly. The manual gives 20 BPM as the example for a circle phaser and notes that at the default 60 BPM the fixture may not keep up.7 In this book’s method, keeping Speed on the song’s BPM and giving a large Measure (4–8 beats, Table 12-2 in Chapter 12) does the same job. A relative wave with a small travel, though, can keep up with a short Measure. The upper moving in Table 25-4 travels only ±10°, so it is set to 2 beats.[Author to confirm]

25.6 Starting Values for Dimmer Phasers

Here are seven commonly used dimmer phasers, with values to start from.

The six other than the comet tail are all the same frame: a two-step snap. Step 1 is 100%, step 2 is 0%, both steps have Transition 0, and step 2’s Width is 100 minus step 1’s Width. Keep the speed on the song’s BPM (the speed master of Chapter 28) and change only Measure.

Snap pulse 1 30 All 0 1.87 times
Odd/even 1 50 Odd 0, even 180 1.87 times
Odd/even, fast 0.5 50 Odd 0, even 180 3.73 times (outside the broadcast limit, Chapter 17)
Running 2 25 Six-way split, 60° each 0.93 times (up to six times across the whole stage)

Sparkle 0.5 22 Random six-way split, 60° each 3.73 times (outside the broadcast limit, Chapter 17)

Choose step 1’s Width between 22 and 50%. The shorter it is, the sharper the hit; the longer, the heavier. The manual gives no range for whether the console accepts a decimal Measure such as 0.5 and plays it as entered, so check once the first time you use it.[Author to confirm] The phase interval is 360 divided by the number of splits (60° for six splits), and the phase of the last slot is 300, not 360. This last-phase trap is covered in Chapter 26.

What changed in 2.5
  • At with a value onlyFrom 2.5.1.0, when the programmer has a phaser, entering just a value without an attribute, like At 50, goes in as a dimmer value (the same after ClearAll). Use this when you want to change only brightness while building a phaser.9
  • The Phaser column of the Sequence SheetThe settings that decide how a phaser stored in a cue starts and at what speed it runs (Sync, Speed Master and so on) are gathered under the Sequence Sheet’s new Phaser column. We look at each setting in Section 27.5.9
  • Names of the speed fields in presetsA preset’s Speed Scale has been renamed Phaser Scale, and Speed Master has been renamed Phaser Speedmaster (Chapter 28).9
  • Phaser recipesThere is now an Edit Recipe mode that builds a phaser as a recipe by pressing a group and a position preset in turn (Chapter 26).9

Follow along

  1. Set one speed master to 112 BPM, and connect the speed of every phaser below to that master (the details are in Chapter 28).
  2. Select the back group, put dimmer 100 in step 1, MA + Next, and dimmer 0 in step 2. Select all steps with MA + Set and give Transition 0. Select only step 1 and give Width 30, and give step 2 Width 70. Enter Measure 1.
  3. On stage, check that the pulse lights once per beat and only for the first 30% of the beat, then store it in an All pool with /Selective. Name it “Low bass every beat.”
  4. Make the other rows of Table 25-4 the same way. For the side hi-hat, select the side left-right alternating group (Chapter 24) and give Phase 0 for stage right and 180 for left.
  5. For the upper moving, select the spot left → right group, make two steps of relative pan +10 and −10, choose sine in Select Form, and spread the phase left → right. Spreading the phase is covered in detail in Chapter 26.
  6. Play the song, call the layers one at a time, and check that each matches the wording of the Chapter 11 grid.
How to check
  1. At 112 BPM, run “Low bass every beat” and count the flashes for 10 seconds with your phone’s stopwatch.
  2. Between 18 and 19 is right (1.87 times × 10 s, Calculation). Around 37 means one cycle went in as half a beat, and around 9 means as two beats, so look at Measure.
  3. If you did not enter Measure, the sum of the Widths becomes one cycle, so look at the Width sum as well.
  4. Check that the pixel claps change color on beats 2 and 4, and that the upper moving swings back and forth once per 2 beats.
Try it with your own song
  1. In the beat grid of the song you are working on, pick two rhythm layers and write their five parameters as in Table 25-4.
  2. Turn the grid’s wording into “every how many beats, how long, in what shape, toward which side” and the five parameters come out.

Key points

  • A phaser has two or more steps. With one step it does not move, even if the console accepts the values.
  • The five parameters are Speed (how fast), Phase (offset), Width (length), Transition (shape) and Measure (beats per cycle). Size is set by the difference between the step values.
  • Flashes per second = BPM ÷ 60 ÷ Measure. It holds only when Measure is entered, when it lights once per cycle, and per single fixture.
  • Sine is Accel·Decel −100; snap is Transition 0. Put the shape in after selecting all the steps.
  • Make position phasers with relative values and start slowly (large Measure).
On the job tomorrow
  1. Before applying an effect, write the beat-grid wording as one line: “every how many beats (Measure), how long (Width), in what shape (Transition), toward which side (Phase).”
  2. That one line is exactly the values to put into the console.
